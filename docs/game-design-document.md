# Game Design Document

## Document status

This is an initial planning document. Items labeled **Confirmed** describe the
project brief; items labeled **Proposal** require team agreement.

## Game overview

**Confirmed:** Eco Choices is an educational serious game about the
environmental impact of everyday decisions. Players experience a simulated
day and make choices across several activity areas.

**Proposal:** A short, replayable day could provide a useful structure for
comparing choices and reflecting on trade-offs.

## Target audience (TBD)

The primary age group, prior knowledge, accessibility needs, and expected
session length are **TBD**.

## Educational objectives

**Confirmed:** The game should raise awareness of sustainability and help
players understand consequences of daily habits.

**Proposal:** Learning objectives may include identifying trade-offs,
explaining why impacts differ, and reflecting on how several small choices
combine over a day.

## Core gameplay loop

**Proposal:**

1. Present a daily situation.
2. Offer several understandable choices.
3. Let the player select and confirm a decision.
4. Update the player's environmental impact summary.
5. Explain the result and continue to the next situation.
6. End with a day summary and reflection prompt.

## Decision-making mechanics

Choices should have understandable descriptions and meaningful trade-offs.
They should avoid implying that a player is morally judged for one decision.
The number of options, whether choices are timed, and whether choices can be
revisited are **TBD**.

Example daily decisions (**Proposal**):

- **Transportation:** Walk, take public transport, cycle, or use a car for a
  commute.
- **Water consumption:** Take a shorter or longer shower, or consider another
  water-use habit.
- **Food:** Choose among meals with different ingredients and preparation
  impacts.
- **Waste:** Repair, reuse, recycle, compost, or dispose of an item when
  appropriate.

## Environmental scoring system

**Confirmed:** Decisions should affect an environmental impact score or
summary.

**TBD:** The categories, weights, data sources, uncertainty, and display of
the score. No scientific formula or numeric value is established here. Any
future model must be researched, documented, and presented with appropriate
limitations.

## Example scenarios

The following are scenario ideas, not finalized content:

- Choosing how to travel to campus.
- Deciding how to use water while getting ready.
- Selecting breakfast or lunch from several options.
- Disposing of packaging after a meal.

## Progression and player feedback

**Proposal:** Progression could follow a morning-to-evening day structure,
with immediate explanations and a final summary. Feedback should explain
trade-offs in plain language and avoid overclaiming precision. Unlocks,
multiple days, achievements, and replay comparison are **TBD**.

## Accessibility considerations

**Proposal:** Plan for readable text, sufficient contrast, keyboard or
alternative input support where applicable, captions or text alternatives for
audio, non-color-only feedback, adjustable timing, and clear language. The
final requirements depend on the chosen platform and target audience.

## Future features

Possible future features include multiple day themes, scenario variations,
classroom discussion prompts, localization, and optional comparison of
different playthroughs. These are not commitments.

## Open design questions

- Who is the target audience and what learning outcomes should be assessed?
- How long should one playthrough take?
- Which impact categories are supported by reliable sources?
- How should uncertainty and trade-offs be communicated?
- Can choices be revisited, and how should replay work?
- What feedback is useful without turning the score into a competition?
- Which accessibility requirements must be supported from the first release?
